\begin{abstract}
The paradigm of Industry 5.0 and smart manufacturing envisions pervasive integration of 6G Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) to drive real-time closed-loop control and autonomous cyber-physical coordination. However, applied machine learning (ML) models evaluating network telemetry frequently suffer from methodological vulnerabilities, including temporal data leakage, target contamination, and unvalidated synthetic benchmark assumptions. In this paper, we propose an end-to-end, leakage-safe temporal-evidence pipeline for auditing network telemetry and quantifying its causal and predictive influence on industrial manufacturing efficiency. Through comprehensive Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) goodness-of-fit testing across nine industrial features, we reveal that the studied enterprise telemetry dataset ($N=100,000$ observations across 50 machines over 68.4 days) is statistically consistent with independent uniform generation; no pairwise feature correlation survives Bonferroni correction across all 36 pairs (maximum $|r|=0.0075$, uncorrected $p=0.0170$, adjusted $p=0.6127$). While a naive segmented regression search suggests a latency breakpoint at 20.7\,ms ($F=4.3127$, naive asymptotic $p=0.0381$), a rigorous supremum-$F$ permutation test ($N=2,000$) reveals $p=0.1549$ (FDR $q=0.5327$), establishing the threshold as exploratory rather than confirmatory. Utilizing a strictly leakage-safe Purged Group Time-Series Split, we benchmark Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM models: network telemetry alone achieves near-chance classification discrimination (ROC-AUC 0.5015--0.5074) for composite efficiency, whereas multimodal models achieve ROC-AUC 0.9427--0.9597 strictly by reconstructing algebraic components of the target label. Our findings highlight the critical necessity of rigorous data audits and leak-free temporal benchmarking in smart factory research.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The Promise: 6G URLLC in Industry 4.0/5.0}
The convergence of advanced telecommunications and industrial automation forms the core infrastructure of Industry 4.0 and 5.0. Future Sixth-Generation (6G) wireless communication networks promise to deliver sub-millisecond round-trip latencies, extreme jitter bounds, and block reliability rates exceeding $99.99999\%$ ($10^{-7}$ block error rate)~\cite{haque2025urllc, ibrahim2025urllc}. In mission-critical cyber-physical production systems (CPPS), such Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) capabilities are designed to underpin closed-loop robotic cell collaboration, distributed programmable logic controllers (PLCs), edge-native computer vision, and real-time automated guided vehicle (AGV) routing~\cite{paudyal2025gnb, alves2021beyond}. 

Standardization bodies, notably 3GPP in Technical Specification TS 22.104~\cite{tgpp22104}, have articulated precise communication service requirements for vertical domains, specifying end-to-end latency tolerances between 0.5\,ms and 20\,ms for motion control and machine interaction. In parallel, smart factory operators increasingly ingest wireless telemetry (e.g., latency, jitter, packet loss) alongside operational technology (OT) machine sensors (e.g., temperature, vibration, power consumption) into predictive artificial intelligence pipelines to forecast production throughput and avoid costly operational downtime~\cite{bousselham2023multimodal}.

\subsection{The Problem: Data Leakage and Optimistic Evaluation}
Despite significant investments in smart manufacturing analytics, a profound reproducibility crisis looms over applied predictive modeling~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}. Recent methodological surveys demonstrate that a dominant fraction of published smart factory and IoT machine learning architectures report inflated classification accuracies due to subtle forms of data leakage~\cite{kaufman2012leakage}. Specifically, two vulnerabilities are rampant:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Autocorrelation and Group Contamination}: Standard random $K$-fold cross-validation mixes temporally adjacent observations across train and test partitions. When machines operate under continuous autoregressive processes, random splits leak temporal dependencies, causing models to memorize specific time-series trajectories rather than generalizing to unseen temporal horizons~\cite{lopez2018advances}.
    \item \textbf{Target Contamination / Proxy Feature Leakage}: Target labels such as ``Overall Equipment Efficiency'' (OEE) or categorical ``Operational Efficiency'' are frequently derived as deterministic or thresholded composite functions of raw operational features (e.g., error counts, defect counts, production rates). Including raw or weakly masked components of the composite label as predictive covariates creates circular tautologies where ML models merely invert the labeling heuristic rather than learning physical degradation phenomena.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Reality Gap: Synthetic Uniform Benchmarks}
Compounding these evaluation failures, researchers and practitioners often benchmark complex predictive algorithms on public or semi-synthetic industrial datasets without verifying the underlying generative process. When synthetic benchmarks are produced via independent pseudorandom uniform sampling, physical interactions—such as network congestion directly degrading production cadence—vanish. 

Treating such datasets as empirical industrial telemetry leads to erroneous conclusions: data-mining algorithms may detect spurious statistical associations, cluster continuous noise into artificial operational ``regimes,'' and report deceptive structural breakpoints.

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
In this paper, we present the design, mathematical formalization, and empirical execution of a leakage-safe temporal-evidence pipeline applied to a major enterprise smart factory telemetry dataset. Our primary contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Generative and Label Auditing}: We conduct formal Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) goodness-of-fit testing across nine continuous telemetry variables and analyze 36 pairwise correlations. We show that the dataset is statistically consistent with independent uniform generation, and formally audit the deterministic composite logic governing efficiency labels.
    \item \textbf{Methodological Finding on Data-Chosen Breakpoints}: We demonstrate that testing data-chosen structural breakpoints using standard naive asymptotic $F$-distributions yields severely inflated type-I errors ($F=4.3127$, naive $p=0.0381$). We implement a vectorized supremum-$F$ permutation test ($N=2,000$) that establishes the empirical threshold ($\tau=20.7$\,ms) as exploratory ($p=0.1549$, FDR $q=0.5327$), presenting this as an essential methodological standard for industrial telemetry analytics.
    \item \textbf{Leakage-Safe Temporal Benchmarking}: We design a 5-fold Purged and Embargoed Group Time-Series Split architecture that eliminates temporal autocorrelation and machine ID leakage. We benchmark Random Forest, XGBoost, and LightGBM across three hierarchical feature tiers (Network-only, Multimodal, Multimodal + Lagged), revealing that network telemetry alone yields near-chance classification (ROC-AUC 0.5015--0.5074).
    \item \textbf{Open-Source Verifiable Pipeline}: We release the entire experimental codebase, automated data auditing suites, interactive Streamlit operational dashboard, and reproducibility scripts as an open-source framework for the industrial informatics community.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Atharva110409/smart-factory-6g-network}}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Network Telemetry in Smart Factories}
The integration of 5G and nascent 6G private cellular networks provides industrial environments with fine-grained telemetry tracking packet latency, radio signal strength indicators (RSSI), and transmission error rates~\cite{haque2025urllc}. Several recent architectures explore localized breakout mechanisms at the gNodeB (gNB) to minimize propagation delays for robotic workcells~\cite{paudyal2025gnb, ibrahim2025urllc}. While communication-theoretic analyses prove that finite blocklength coding limits packet reliability under extreme deadlines~\cite{popovski20185g}, practical implementations often struggle to correlate transient packet fluctuations with macroscopic machine states due to low signal-to-noise ratios and buffer masking.

\subsection{Predictive Maintenance and Methodological Pitfalls}
In the domain of predictive maintenance (PdM), tree-based gradient boosting algorithms (XGBoost, LightGBM) and Random Forests remain dominant due to their robust handling of tabular, heterogeneous sensor feeds~\cite{breiman2001random, chen2016xgboost, ke2017lightgbm}. However, as documented by Kaufman \textit{et al.}~\cite{kaufman2012leakage} and Kapoor and Narayanan~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}, predictive modeling in industrial informatics frequently suffers from proxy feature leakage. When machine efficiency is defined via composite scoring rules that directly aggregate individual telemetry streams, standard cross-validation fails to detect that the classifier is simply reproducing the arithmetic definition of the target. To our knowledge, this study is the first to integrate formal distribution audits, non-parametric permutation breakpoint testing, and purged group cross-validation into a unified smart factory telemetry pipeline.

\subsection{Dataset Provenance and Statistical Distribution Audit}
The case study analyzes the enterprise-scale \textit{Thales Group Manufacturing Telemetry Benchmark}, capturing continuous smart factory operations. The dataset comprises $N=100,000$ consecutive records sampled at regular 1-minute intervals across 50 distinct industrial CNC machines and robotic cells, spanning exactly 68.44 days (from 2025-01-01 00:00:00 to 2025-03-10 10:39:00). Each record logs timestamp, machine identifier ($M \in [1, 50]$), discrete operating mode ($S \in \{\text{Active}, \text{Idle}, \text{Maintenance}\}$), two wireless network telemetry features, and six operational/environmental sensor variables.

To address \textbf{RQ1}, we test whether each continuous telemetry variable $X$ originates from an authentic physical stochastic process or is statistically indistinguishable from a continuous uniform distribution $\mathcal{U}(a, b)$, where $a = \min(X)$ and $b = \max(X)$. We apply the two-sided one-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test:
\begin{equation}
D_N = \sup_{x \in [a, b]} \left| F_N(x) - F_{\mathcal{U}}(x) \right|,
\label{eq:ks_test}
\end{equation}
where $F_N(x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}_{x_i \le x}$ is the empirical cumulative distribution function (ECDF), and $F_{\mathcal{U}}(x) = \frac{x - a}{b - a}$ is the theoretical uniform CDF. 

Furthermore, to test for multivariate linear dependencies, we compute the Pearson correlation matrix $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 9}$ across all $\binom{9}{2} = 36$ distinct feature pairs and apply the family-wise Bonferroni correction:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{adj}} = \min\left(1.0, \; 36 \times p_{\text{uncorr}}\right).
\label{eq:bonferroni}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Label Construction Audit and Net Representation Index}
The operational target in the benchmark is categorical \textit{Operational Efficiency}, categorized into three ordinal levels: $\mathcal{Y} \in \{\text{Low}, \text{Medium}, \text{High}\}$. Auditing the ground truth against operational telemetry reveals that $\mathcal{Y}$ is governed by a deterministic composite decision boundary:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Y} = 
\begin{cases}
\text{Low}, & \text{if } Q > 5.0 \;\lor\; E > 10.0 \;\lor\; P < 200, \\
\text{Medium}, & \text{else if } Q > 3.0 \;\lor\; E > 5.0 \;\lor\; P < 350, \\
\text{High}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:label_rule}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ denotes Quality Control Defect Rate (\%), $E$ denotes Error Rate (\%), and $P$ denotes Production Speed (units/hr). 

Recomputing the exact class frequencies across all 100,000 records confirms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Low Efficiency}: $N_{\text{Low}} = 77,825$ ($77.825\%$)
    \item \textbf{Medium Efficiency}: $N_{\text{Med}} = 19,189$ ($19.189\%$)
    \item \textbf{High Efficiency}: $N_{\text{High}} = 2,986$ ($2.986\%$)
\end{itemize}
To quantify the extreme class skew, we compute the Net Representation Index (NRI) between the majority and minority classes:
\begin{equation}
\text{NRI}_{\text{Low vs High}} = \frac{N_{\text{Low}} - N_{\text{High}}}{N_{\text{Low}} + N_{\text{High}}} = \frac{77,825 - 2,986}{77,825 + 2,986} = 0.9261.
\label{eq:nri}
\end{equation}
This verifies severe imbalance: over $77.8\%$ of operational states fall into the degraded ``Low'' efficiency regime, while pristine ``High'' efficiency represents less than $3\%$ of operations.

\subsection{Temporal Stationarity and Causal Precedence}
To address \textbf{RQ2}, we evaluate the time-series dynamics of network latency ($L_t$) and production speed ($P_t$). We first test for the presence of a unit root using the Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) regression~\cite{dickey1979distribution}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta Y_t = \alpha + \beta t + \gamma Y_{t-1} + \sum_{j=1}^p \delta_j \Delta Y_{t-j} + \varepsilon_t.
\label{eq:adf}
\end{equation}
Rejecting the null hypothesis $H_0: \gamma = 0$ confirms weak stationarity.

Next, we test whether network latency Granger-causes production speed across temporal lags $k \in \{1, 2, \dots, 15\}$ minutes~\cite{granger1969investigating}. We estimate the bivariate autoregressive model:
\begin{equation}
P_t = c + \sum_{i=1}^k a_i P_{t-i} + \sum_{i=1}^k b_i L_{t-i} + u_t,
\label{eq:granger}
\end{equation}
and test the joint restriction $H_0: b_1 = b_2 = \dots = b_k = 0$ using an $F$-test. To guard against inflated false discovery rates across multiple tested horizons, we apply the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) procedure~\cite{benjamini1995controlling}, enforcing an FDR threshold of $q = 0.05$.

\subsection{Structural Breakpoint Detection: Naive $F$ vs. Sup-$F$ Permutation}
To address \textbf{RQ3}, we examine whether network latency exhibits a sharp threshold beyond which production speed collapses. A segmented linear regression model parameterizes this relationship across candidate breakpoint $\tau \in [\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]$:
\begin{equation}
P_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 L_t + \beta_2 (L_t - \tau) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{L_t > \tau\}} + \varepsilon_t.
\label{eq:segmented_regression}
\end{equation}
Testing the presence of a structural break evaluates the null hypothesis $H_0: \beta_2 = 0$ against $H_1: \beta_2 \ne 0$. In standard software implementations, an exhaustive grid search selects $\hat{\tau} = \arg\max_\tau F(\tau)$, where $F(\tau)$ is the Chow-type $F$-statistic comparing the segmented model against a simple linear baseline.

\textbf{Methodological Hazard}: As proved by Andrews~\cite{andrews1993tests} and Hansen~\cite{hansen2000sample}, when the threshold parameter $\tau$ is unknown under the null hypothesis (nuisance parameter problem), the test statistic $\sup_{\tau} F(\tau)$ does \textit{not} follow a standard Fisher-Snedecor $F_{1, N-k}$ distribution. Selecting the maximal $F$-statistic across a dense grid guarantees that the naive $p$-value will be substantially anti-conservative.

To obtain an exact, finite-sample valid test without relying on asymptotic approximations, we implement a vectorized supremum-$F$ permutation test with $B = 2,000$ iterations. Utilizing the Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (FWL) theorem, we project out the nuisance subspace $\mathbf{X}_0 = [\mathbf{1}, \mathbf{L}]$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_0 = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{X}_0 (\mathbf{X}_0^T \mathbf{X}_0)^{-1} \mathbf{X}_0^T.
\label{eq:projection}
\end{equation}
For each candidate $\tau$, the orthogonalized segmented regressor is $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_\tau = \mathbf{M}_0 (\mathbf{L} - \tau)_+$. For any permuted response vector $\mathbf{P}^{(b)}$, the numerator of the $F$-statistic reduces to a fast matrix-vector dot product:
\begin{equation}
F^{(b)}(\tau) = \frac{(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_\tau^T \mathbf{P}^{(b)})^2 / (\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_\tau^T \tilde{\mathbf{z}}_\tau)}{\hat{\sigma}_{(b)}^2(\tau)}.
\label{eq:vectorized_f}
\end{equation}
The empirical supremum permutation $p$-value is computed as:
\begin{equation}
p_{\text{perm}} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \mathbf{1}_{\left\{ \sup_\tau F^{(b)}(\tau) \ge \sup_\tau F_{\text{obs}}(\tau) \right\}}.
\label{eq:permutation_pval}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Leakage-Safe Predictive Modeling Framework}
To answer \textbf{RQ5}, we formulate the prediction of operational efficiency $\mathcal{Y}_t$ as a supervised multiclass classification task. 

\subsubsection{Feature Tiers}
To evaluate the true marginal contribution of network telemetry and expose proxy feature leakage, we benchmark three strictly defined feature tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier A (Network Only)}: Includes only network latency ($L_t$), packet loss rate, machine identifier, and operating mode.
    \item \textbf{Tier B (Multimodal)}: Expands Tier A to include all operational machine telemetry: temperature, vibration, power consumption, maintenance score, production speed, error rate, and defect rate.
    \item \textbf{Tier C (Multimodal + Lagged)}: Expands Tier B with backward-looking autoregressive features (lags $t-1, t-2, t-5$) and rolling statistical summaries (mean, standard deviation over 15-minute and 60-minute windows).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Purged and Embargoed Group Time-Series Split}
Standard cross-validation leaks information when applied to time series. To enforce strict leakage safety, we construct a 5-fold \texttt{PurgedGroupTimeSeriesSplit} adapting L{\'o}pez de Prado's framework~\cite{lopez2018advances}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Directionality}: All training windows strictly precede testing windows ($t_{\text{train}} < t_{\text{test}}$).
    \item \textbf{Purging and Embargoing}: An embargo buffer of $T_{\text{embargo}} = 30$ minutes is stripped immediately following every training split to eliminate serial autoregressive spillover.
    \item \textbf{Group Constraints}: Machine identifiers ($\text{Machine\_ID}$) are tracked to ensure that observations from identical physical units do not bridge fold boundaries during embargo transitions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Model Architectures and Evaluation Metrics}
We benchmark three state-of-the-art non-linear tabular learning architectures: Random Forest (100 estimators)~\cite{breiman2001random}, XGBoost (histogram-based gradient boosting)~\cite{chen2016xgboost}, and LightGBM~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}, alongside a naive Dummy Most-Frequent baseline. 

Given the severe class imbalance ($\text{NRI} = 0.9261$), accuracy is clinically misleading. We report:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{One-vs-Rest (OVR) Macro ROC-AUC}
    \item \textbf{OVR Macro PR-AUC} (Precision-Recall Area)
    \item \textbf{Macro F1-Score}
    \item \textbf{Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC)}:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{MCC} = \frac{c \cdot s - \sum_k p_k t_k}{\sqrt{(s^2 - \sum_k p_k^2)(s^2 - \sum_k t_k^2)}},
    \label{eq:mcc}
    \end{equation}
    where $c$ is the total correct predictions, $s$ is the total samples, $p_k$ is the row marginal, and $t_k$ is the column marginal.
    \item \textbf{Multiclass Brier Score Loss} and \textbf{Log Loss}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Cost-Benefit Matrix Analysis}
To translate classification performance into tangible operational return-on-investment (ROI), we define an asymmetric cost matrix reflective of smart factory dispatch economics:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C} = 
\begin{pmatrix}
C_{\text{TP}} & C_{\text{FP}} \\
C_{\text{FN}} & C_{\text{TN}}
\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\$0 & \$250 \\
\$2,500 & \$0
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cost_matrix}
\end{equation}
Here, a False Negative ($C_{\text{FN}} = \$2,500$) represents catastrophic unpredicted downtime resulting in line stoppage, whereas a False Positive ($C_{\text{FP}} = \$250$) represents the minor operational cost of an unnecessary secondary diagnostic inspection. The expected operational loss per sample is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[\text{Cost}] = \frac{1}{N} \left( C_{\text{FP}} \cdot N_{\text{FP}} + C_{\text{FN}} \cdot N_{\text{FN}} \right).
\label{eq:expected_cost}
\end{equation}

\section{Empirical Results}

\subsection{Statistical Distribution and Multicollinearity Audit (RQ1)}

Thus, all nine features are \textit{statistically consistent with independent uniform generation}.

Furthermore, evaluation of the pairwise correlation matrix $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 9}$ reveals that all 36 pairwise Pearson correlation coefficients are extraordinarily close to zero ($|r| < 0.0076$). \end{equation}
Not a single correlation survives multiple testing correction. There is no evidence of cross-feature coupling in the raw telemetry.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Answers to Research Questions}
We systematically address the five research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Generative Distribution)}: Formal KS tests fail to reject continuous uniformity for all nine telemetry variables after Bonferroni correction ($p_{\text{adj}} \ge 0.1082$). Pairwise correlations are statistically negligible ($|r| < 0.0076$, none survive Bonferroni across 36 pairs). The telemetry is statistically consistent with independent uniform generation.
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Temporal Causality)}: Telemetry streams are weakly stationary ($p < 0.001$), but Granger causality tests across lags 1--15 reveal no causal precedence of latency over production speed ($p > 0.42$, FDR $q > 0.05$).
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Breakpoint Validity)}: The apparent 20.7\,ms latency breakpoint is a data-mining artifact ($F=4.3127$, naive $p=0.0381$). A rigorous supremum-$F$ permutation test reveals $p=0.1549$ (FDR $q=0.5327$). The breakpoint is exploratory.
    \item \textbf{RQ4 (Network Regimes)}: $K$-means clustering achieves a modest silhouette score of $0.3341$, reflecting an arbitrary geometric partitioning of a continuous uniform bivariate space rather than discrete physical states.
    \item \textbf{RQ5 (Predictive Power and Leakage)}: Under leak-free temporal splitting, network telemetry achieves near-chance classification (ROC-AUC $\approx 0.505$). High performance in multimodal models ($>0.95$) is entirely driven by reconstructing the algebraic components of the target label.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Methodological Hazard of Data-Chosen Breakpoints}
Our empirical demonstration contrasting the naive $F$-test ($p=0.0381$) against the permutation test ($p=0.1549$) illustrates a fundamental methodological lesson for industrial informatics. In sensor telemetry, scanning for operational degradation thresholds across continuous grids is common practice. When the threshold is chosen to maximize fit, standard asymptotic $F$-distributions no longer apply. Without permutation resampling or Hansen's sample-splitting corrections~\cite{hansen2000sample}, researchers risk publishing spurious thresholds that fail to replicate in live manufacturing environments.

\subsection{The Continuum Partitioning Fallacy}
A parallel hazard occurs in unsupervised network profiling. Applying clustering algorithms (e.g., $K$-means, GMMs) to uniform or uncoupled bivariate telemetry will always yield mathematical partitions. When Calinski-Harabasz metrics are inflated by large sample sizes ($N=100,000$), analysts may mistake these sectors for authentic network Quality of Service (QoS) classes. Rigorous validation against null uniform reference models (e.g., via gap statistics or permutation) is mandatory.

\subsection{Alignment with 3GPP TS 22.104 and Operational Reality}
Standards such as 3GPP TS 22.104~\cite{tgpp22104} mandate latencies below 1--10\,ms for motion control. In authentic factory environments, exceeding these limits causes PLC buffer overflows, emergency packet drops, and physical machine stops. In the analyzed synthetic benchmark, however, latency fluctuates uniformly between 1\,ms and 50\,ms without impacting machine physics. This highlights the urgent need for authentic open-access OT/IT datasets captured from physical testbeds.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
The primary limitation of this case study is that the underlying dataset is synthetic. While the benchmark provides realistic enterprise schemas, its uniform feature distributions prevent the emergence of genuine cyber-physical feedback loops. Our analysis does not imply that 6G URLLC is irrelevant to smart factories; rather, it proves that the benchmark dataset cannot support causal inferences regarding network QoS.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented an end-to-end, leakage-safe temporal-evidence pipeline for smart factory telemetry. Through rigorous statistical audits, we established that the benchmark dataset is statistically consistent with independent uniform generation, disconfirmed a naive 20.7\,ms latency breakpoint via supremum-$F$ permutation testing ($p=0.1549$), and demonstrated that network telemetry alone yields near-chance predictive power (ROC-AUC $\approx 0.505$) under leak-free temporal cross-validation. 

Our methodological findings underscore the critical importance of auditing generative distributions, guarding against proxy feature leakage, and validating data-chosen changepoints. Future work will deploy this open-source pipeline on physical, hardware-in-the-loop 5G/6G industrial testbeds to capture authentic non-linear network-physical interactions.
